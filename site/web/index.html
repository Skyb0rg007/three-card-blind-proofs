    About this game

    This is the 3-Card Blind matchup modelled in the three-card-blind-proofs repository. Both players see everything, start at 20 life and start with their three cards in hand. City of Traitors is treated as already on the battlefield (RULES.md S1). When Elixir shuffles B's library, A chooses the order, because 3CB resolves random effects in the way most favourable to the opponent.

    The rules engine is the repository's Rust port of the Rocq model (theories/Model.v), compiled unchanged to WebAssembly. It is checked against the native solver's differential walk, byte for byte.

    How strong is the computer?

    The computer plays a bounded search, not proven-optimal play. It searches every decision of the current turn and several more turns, then scores the position with a heuristic: life totals, counters, Thopters, cards, and lethal threats. The real game is unsolved. The solver's tables run to tens of millions of positions and several gigabytes, so they can't be shipped to a browser.

    What the research says so far

    • From the opening where A goes first, the solver shows A cannot force a win: B survives from 20 life.
    • A does win if B starts with 15 life or less.
    • B is believed to need about 25 life to force a win.
    • So the real game is believed to be a draw, but that is unproven on B's side.

    Walkers can grow forever and Elixir can loop, so games can go on indefinitely. The page offers a draw when a position repeats or the game runs long, and you can declare one at any time.
